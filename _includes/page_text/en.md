### My Journey
Now, I would like to talk a little more about my journey up until now. I was born in 1998 in Unaí, a small city close to Brazil's capital, Brasília. Since I was a kid, I've always liked to learn how things are built and how they work, from machines to nature itself, and even how humans behave in certain activities (like rules, history, and so on). In other words, I'm a curious person. I have also always loved math: seeing big equations being developed on a page, following each step, each transformation; for me, there's beauty in that. So, when I discovered programming, I thought I would love it, even though I had no computer background. However, at first, that wasn't the case.

I lived in Unaí until I was 15 years old, when I moved to Uberlândia to attend high school in a bigger city. During those years, I had my first contact with programming, taking a 4-week training course or something similar at UFU (which would later become my university). After that first experience, I liked programming, but I decided to focus on studying to get into college, which I did, enrolling in Computer Science in 2017.

In my first year of college, I learned C and Prolog, and it wasn't great. I had some difficulties at the beginning and almost decided to quit and change my major, but deep down I saw something I might like, so I decided to give it another try. By the middle of the second year, I was loving it. I joined PET (a government program that encourages research and extracurricular activities to complement academic training), and there I met people who gave their energy and time to teach me, turning my passion for programming into something real. I also discovered Python, a language with a more human-friendly syntax, which helped me understand how programming works and how much I enjoy it. Nowadays, when I come across a more complex language, it's not as hard, because in the end it's just an algorithm, a set of steps, just like math.

With that, I knew I liked computers and wanted to work with something related, but what? Data Science: the field that brings together mathematics/statistics, computer science/programming and business knowledge. The first I've liked since I was a kid, the second I started to like during college, and the third, if you stop to think about it, is related to human behavior in certain activities, something I enjoy as well. Because of that, since college, I've enjoyed spending time improving my knowledge in these three areas.

During my time in PET, I was able to teach computer topics to younger students, do research in bioinformatics and work with a large group of people (more than 10). I also took part in lectures, meetings and other events, as well as some university games, competing in sports like volleyball and judo, sometimes even helping with the organization. My first internship and a student exchange to Egypt also happened during college.

I finished college in 2021 and already had a job. In 2022, I moved to São Paulo. I had always wanted to know what it was like to live there: since I'm from a small city, I wanted the experience of living in a big one, so I chose the biggest city in South America, and I loved it. But after two years, I was looking for more, so in 2024 I decided to leave São Paulo behind and became a rambler.

I did that because I want to meet more people and see how other societies work, their similarities and especially their differences (I love the latter; it attracts me. I like to get involved in things that are not normal for me: figuring them out, understanding them and seeing them through my own eyes). Since I was working remotely, that was possible, so I put everything I needed in a backpack (I left my important belongings with my parents) and started going wherever the winds told me I should and could go.

After finishing college, I spent almost five years at LexisNexis: first on ETL pipelines, then building real-time data products on the Roxie team. At the end of 2025 I decided to step away for a while, to rest, travel more slowly and get back to what made me choose this field in the first place: **data**.

So 2026 is my sabbatical year. I'm using it to recharge, refresh my stack and build real projects along the way. I'm coming back with a clear intent: to work with data again.
